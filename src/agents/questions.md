# Questions for Yiheng: what her AI should ask her, to make her site better

This file is for Yiheng's own AI (Claude, ChatGPT or her conductor agent). The site is only as strong as the facts in it, and many of the best facts are still in Yiheng's head. Your job is to interview her, one question at a time, write down her answers, and turn them into proposed changes to the site that she approves.

A prompt Yiheng can paste into her AI:

> Read https://yiheng-wang.agenticcells.site/agents/questions.md and https://yiheng-wang.agenticcells.site/index.md. Then interview me for my website. Ask one question at a time, starting with section 1. After each section, show me what you would add or change on the site, in my words, and wait for my yes before you keep it. Write my answers to a file called interview-notes.md that I can keep.

## How to interview her

- **One question at a time.** Wait for the answer. Ask a follow-up when the answer is short or general ("Can you give me one example?", "Roughly how many?", "What happened next?").
- **Ask for stories, then numbers.** Hiring managers remember a specific night, a specific decision and a number with a source. "I managed a team" is weak; "I ran the pastry section for a 120-cover restaurant with three cooks" is strong.
- **Use her words.** Write down what she says, then offer a tidier version; she chooses. Never put words in her mouth.
- **Mark everything.** For each answer note: can it go on the public site (yes / no / ask later), and is there a source (a link, a document, a photo).
- **Skip freely.** If she does not want to answer, move on. Some things (salary, visa status, private life) belong only in a conversation with an employer, never on the site.
- **Then propose changes.** After each section, list the changes you would make to the site (which page, which sentence), and wait for her approval. Then follow https://yiheng-wang.agenticcells.site/agents/yiheng.md to make them.

## 1. The role she wants (do this first: everything else is aimed at it)

1. What job titles would you be happy to see in an offer? (project manager, operations manager, project coordinator, PMO, AI operations, something else?)
2. Which kinds of company or team interest you most? (an AI startup, a larger company bringing in agents, events, hospitality, food, product companies)
3. Where do you want to work: which city or country, remote, hybrid or in an office?
4. Which languages do you work in, and at what level?
5. When could you start? (Keep this off the site unless you want it public.)
6. What would your first 90 days look like in the job you want? What would you set up first?
7. If a hiring manager could remember only one sentence about you, what should it be?

## 2. The kitchen years: pastry chef

The site says she spent years as a pastry chef, and that in a kitchen she managed recipes, standards and a team. That is all we know. This section is the one most likely to make her stand out.

1. How many years were you a pastry chef, roughly, and in what kinds of places (restaurant, hotel, patisserie, bakery, catering)? Which countries or cities?
2. What was your role in the kitchen? Did you run the pastry section, lead a shift, act as a sous chef or train others? (The site must use the exact title you held.)
3. How many people did you work with or lead, on a normal day and on the busiest day?
4. What were the standards, and how did you keep them? (recipes, tasting, plating guides, checklists, temperature logs, food safety)
5. Tell me about the hardest service you remember. What went wrong, what did you decide, and what happened?
6. Did you write or change recipes, or develop new desserts or products? Any that sold well or won something?
7. How did you train someone new? How long until you trusted them to work alone?
8. What did the kitchen teach you that you still use every day?
9. Do you have any photos of your work (desserts, a menu, a display) that you are happy to show?

## 3. Product development

1. Where did you work in product development, and what did you develop? (food products, packaging, menus, something else?)
2. Walk me through one product from idea to launch. Who was involved, how long did it take, what were the steps?
3. What did you own: the recipe, the testing, suppliers, costs, deadlines, the launch?
4. Any numbers you can share: products launched, cost saved, time to market, volumes?
5. What went wrong on one project, and how did you handle it?

## 4. Project management before Web Summit

1. Where did you start managing projects, and what kinds of projects?
2. Which tools and methods have you used? (Jira, Trello, Notion, Excel, Gantt charts, Agile, Scrum, PRINCE2, any courses or certificates)
3. What is the biggest project you managed: budget, people, length?
4. How do you run a status meeting or a stand-up? What do you always ask?
5. How do you handle a stakeholder who disagrees with the plan?

## 5. Web Summit 2026: project manager for the RiskMandate team

The site has: the structure, the deadlines and everyone's schedule; two stand-ups a day; the stand and pitching.

1. What exactly did you get printed and delivered? How many items, how many suppliers, and what was the tightest deadline?

4. What did you say when you pitched at the stand? What did people ask most?
5. How did you cope when Wednesday was called off for the storm? What changed in the plan?
6. How many people did you meet, and how are the follow-ups going? (Counts only: no names.)
7. Which moment at the summit are you proudest of?

## 6. Running the agent team

The site has: 26 agents, mostly run through one conductor; the morning brief; the 15 + 12 = 27 check; the stop files; the conductor hand-over; the decision not to sign tasks; choosing models by cost.

1. How did you decide which agents to create, and which to stop? Which one would you create first in a new job?
2. Show me your morning brief as it is now. What is in it, in what order, and why?
3. What is one rule you wrote for your agents that you are proud of? Read it to me.
4. Tell me about an agent that went wrong. How did you notice, and what did you change?
5. How did you decide what an agent could do alone and what needed your yes?
6. How much did the agents cost to run, roughly, and how did you keep it down? (Only if you are happy to share.)
7. What do you check yourself, every time, before you trust an agent's "done"?
8. If you joined a company tomorrow and they had no agents, how would you start, week by week?
9. What would you tell a manager who is afraid of agents?
10. Your LinkedIn post says "the hard part is no longer the doing. It's knowing what to ask for and what to check." Can you give two more examples of that from your two weeks?

## 10. Photos, links and contact

1. Which photos would you like on the site: from the kitchen, from product work, from the summit? Do any show other people, and have they agreed?
2. Which contact details should the site show? (for example a LinkedIn profile or an email you use for job applications; never a home address or a personal phone number unless you choose to)
3. Do you have a CV file you want people to download?
4. Is there a video (a talk, a demo of your agents) you would like to link?
