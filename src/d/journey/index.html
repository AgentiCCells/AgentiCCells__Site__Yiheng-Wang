The kitchen

Pastry chef in Sydney and Shenzhen, 2015 to 2022I

Yiheng's working life started in a pastry kitchen. Pastry is the least forgiving corner of any kitchen: a recipe is a specification, a gram matters, timing is everything, and the plate that leaves has to match the one before it.

From 2020 to 2022 she was head pastry chef at Daily Bakery in Shenzhen. She led a team of four producing for three shops, ran a new-product launch every quarter, and set the daily checklists that passed every health inspection. On the busiest day, Halloween, the team of four made about 400 to 500 cupcakes and around 20 cake orders.Y

“In a kitchen I managed recipes, standards and a team. Now I manage agents and rules. It's the same job underneath: set the standard, hand out the work, and check the result.”
Yiheng Wang, PrompTugal.news interviewI

How she works

  • “Most of my time went on deciding what to do next and checking what the agents said they had done.”L
  • “If you don't understand a technical choice, ask for the options and the reasons. Then decide.”I
  • “Don't add a rule as soon as you think of it. Talk it through with your conductor first.”I
  • She managed at two altitudes: direction where the work was outside her expertise, depth where she knew best, in editorial judgement. In Dinis Cruz's words, “she became a manager, not a micromanager”.N1

The conductor hand-over

The old conductor started its own successor. She said "go" and sent one line to her vault agent. About an hour, and nothing lost.I

The stop files

After the summit one researcher ran twice more after a stop. She switched off all 25 scheduled tasks by hand, then fixed the cause: every agent now checks four stop files at the start of each run.I

A security decision

Sign tasks or not? She weighed what had actually happened against the team's Agent Behaviour Policies, chose not to sign, and added a lighter safeguard: a weekly check that no rule changed without her word.I

The right model for the job

She moved lighter tasks from Opus to Sonnet, choosing the model to fit the work rather than using the largest for everything.I

A brief that fits how she works

She had her morning brief rebuilt: her actions first, the most urgent at the top, each with where to go, what to reply and why.I

“First I work out what the job actually needs me to do, where I sit in the company, and who I work with. For a project, I get as much material as I can: the background, the result they want, and what counts as done. Every day there's a morning brief and a short stand-up about blockers and problems. Every meeting must end with action items; otherwise it probably didn't need to happen.”
Yiheng Wang, on starting a new jobY

Habits from the kitchen she still uses

  • Group similar tasks. “At Un P'tit Truc I cut the morning production from 2 hours to 1h40 by grouping similar tasks: while one cream whipped, I measured the next and piped the last.”
  • Manage by the result. “With experienced people I only describe the result I want; the order of the steps can be different. Beginners watch me once. After two or three times with no problems, they work alone.”
  • Check a sample. “Each day we picked one product to check before it went out.”
  • Plan first, and don't panic. “Before I start, I run through the direction in my head, so I know roughly how it will go. And when something happens, don't panic. Everything can be solved.”
