How she works

Set the standard, hand out the work, check the result

She manages at two altitudes: direction for agents outside her expertise, depth where she knows best, in editorial judgement. In Dinis Cruz's words, "she became a manager, not a micromanager".

  1. Set the standard

    Rules are talked through before they are added: "Don't add a rule as soon as you think of it. Talk it through with your conductor first."

  2. Hand out the work

    One conductor, one morning brief: "Start with one conductor and only talk to that one. Let it hand out the work."

  3. Check the result

    "When an agent says it's done, it usually is, but not always. I still check a few things myself."

The conductor hand-over

The old conductor started its successor itself. She said "go" and sent one line to her vault agent. About an hour, and nothing lost.

The stop files

After the summit one researcher ran twice more after a stop. She switched off all 25 scheduled tasks by hand, then fixed the cause: every agent now checks four stop files at the start of each run.

A security decision

Signed tasks or not? She weighed what had actually happened against the team's Agent Behaviour Policies, chose not to sign, and added a lighter safeguard: a weekly check by her security agent that no rule changed without her word.

Cost

She moved lighter tasks from Opus to Sonnet, picking the model to fit the job. "If you don't understand a technical choice, ask for the options and the reasons. Then decide."

Reports that fit her

She rebuilt her morning brief: her actions first, the most urgent at the top, each with where to go, what to reply and why.

Asking for help

Her agents worked with the rest of the team's agents through shared channels on one team site; when stuck, she asked Dinis's agents for help.

Sources for these stories: her interview (2026-11-14) and Don't micromanage your agents (2026-11-15).
