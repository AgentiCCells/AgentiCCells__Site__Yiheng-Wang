## How she works

> "In a kitchen I managed recipes, standards and a team. Now I manage agents and rules. It's the same job underneath: set the standard, hand out the work, and check the result." [I]

Her decisions, from her approved interview [I]:

- **Check the work.** She asked her reviewer agent to move the main image in every published article. It reported 15. She opened some herself, found two storm articles it had missed, and sent them back: 12 more were found, 27 in all. "When an agent says it's done, it usually is, but not always. I still check a few things myself."
- **Reports that fit how she works.** She rebuilt her morning brief: her actions first, the most urgent at the top, each with where to go, what to reply and why.
- **A security decision.** Her security agent recommended signing every task. She weighed what had actually happened and the team's Agent Behaviour Policies, chose not to sign, and added a lighter safeguard: a weekly check that no rule changed without her word.
- **The stop files.** After the summit one agent ran twice more after a stop. She switched off all 25 scheduled tasks by hand, then fixed the cause: every agent now checks four stop files at the start of each run.
- **The hand-over.** When her conductor's session had to be replaced, the old conductor started its successor itself. She said "go"; it took about an hour, and nothing was lost.
- **Cost.** She moved lighter work from Opus to Sonnet, and picks the model to fit the job.

Her advice to a colleague starting with agents tomorrow [I]: "Start with one conductor and only talk to that one. Let it hand out the work." "If you don't understand a technical choice, ask for the options and the reasons. Then decide." "Don't add a rule as soon as you think of it. Talk it through with your conductor first." "And you don't need a technical background to start. I don't have one either. Two weeks ago I was starting from zero."

How she starts in a new job [Y]: "First I work out what the job actually needs me to do, where I sit in the company, and who I work with. For a project, I get as much material as I can: the background, the result they want, and what counts as done. Every day there's a morning brief and a short stand-up about blockers and problems. Every meeting must end with action items; otherwise it probably didn't need to happen."

Habits from the kitchen she still uses [Y] (some answers translated from Mandarin):

- **Group similar tasks.** "At Un P'tit Truc I cut the morning production from 2 hours to 1h40 by grouping similar tasks: while one cream whipped, I measured the next and piped the last."
- **Manage by the result.** "With experienced people I only describe the result I want; the order of the steps can be different. Beginners watch me once. After two or three times with no problems, they work alone."
- **Check a sample.** "Each day we picked one product to check before it went out."
- **Plan first, and don't panic.** "Before I start, I run through the direction in my head, so I know roughly how it will go. And when something happens, don't panic. Everything can be solved."

## AI governance in practice

Responsible AI in daily work, on top of her project management. [Y]

- **Human oversight.** Agents draft and a person signs off on outbound work. A reviewer agent checks every claim, and she approves judgement calls. Human-in-the-loop by design. [CV][Y]
- **Standing rules.** 20+ AI agents run under one coordinating agent, with about 90 written rules on who does what and when to escalate to a person. [CV][Y]
- **Access control.** Private material stays in encrypted vaults. Each agent only gets the keys it needs, and a security agent checks rules and access every week. [CV][Y]
- **Audit trail.** Every decision is logged with its date. [LI][Y]
- **Continuous improvement.** She added verification steps after agents reported work as done too early. Risk and issue tracking, then change management. [LI][Y]
- **Source audit.** Re-checked 206 claims on 101 company pages in two days: 47 re-sourced, 159 removed, 98 pages republished. [CV][Y]
- **Agent Behaviour Policies.** Tested RiskMandate's agent behaviour policy tool on riskmandate.ai 18 times and gave feedback, using its reach, mandate and "what stops it" framework. [CV][Y]
